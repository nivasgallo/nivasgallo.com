Betfair
Odds Boost.

A daily boost that lets customers choose which bet to reward, turning loyalty from a promotion into something they control.

Role
Senior UX Designer
Team
Sole designer, with Product and engineering
Platform
Betfair Sportsbook, mobile
Year
2017

Results from the 15-day trial

  • 85%customer satisfaction
  • 116kboosts redeemed
  • £1.9min boosted betting volume
  • Very highease-of-use score

Source: Betfair UK and Ireland customer insights.

The problem.

Sports betting is crowded, and it's hard for one brand to stand out from another. Rewarding loyalty is the second biggest driver of customer engagement, but when we asked customers about Betfair's loyalty offer, it was clear we were losing people after they signed up.

  • Money-back promotions weren't turning into engagement.
  • A lack of reward for loyalty was a core reason customers left a brand, and a reason they chose a new one.
  • Offers were tied to a sport, an event or a time. Customers wanted flexibility.
  • Each offer had different terms and conditions, which confused people.

The idea.

Let customers boost the price of any selection they choose. One boost a day, on the bet that matters to them, instead of an offer we pick for them.

The aim was a loyalty experience built on three feelings: control, excitement, and generosity that competitors didn't match.

Test it before designing it.

Before designing the full feature, we built a simple mockup of the bet slip and tested it with customers. The journey was short and focused on the moment of placing a bet. We were checking six things:

  • Customers notice that a boost is available.
  • It's easy to learn what it does.
  • It's simple to turn on and off.
  • It's clear which price has been boosted.
  • The extra returns are visible.
  • Transitions draw attention to the change in odds and returns.

The test validated the concept, so we moved on to mapping the whole experience.

Map the whole journey.

Odds Boost touched a lot of the product: single bets and multiples, Fastbet, the bet slip, odds movement, and other promotions like Edge. Before designing screens, I needed a bird's-eye view of how it would interact with all of them.

With the product managers and engineering teams, I mapped every route from activation to a placed bet in one user flow, and validated it with stakeholders before any detailed design started.

Prototype the real thing.

From the flow, I built a prototype of the full journey: a quick onboarding card, then a real bet, boosting Liverpool's odds from 2.4 to 2.8 against Leicester.

Customer feedback shaped how the feature behaved. Because the mockup and prototype both tested well, we didn't need many variants. The changes were about clarity:

  • Denser layout. More information in less space on a crowded bet slip.
  • Stronger visual cues. The elements that change, odds and returns, stand out when the boost is applied.
  • A natural button order. Apply Boost, stake, Add to Betslip and Place Bet follow the order customers read the screen.

We then designed the full scope, including edge cases and how Odds Boost worked alongside other features.

How we measured it.

We set three measures of success before launch:

  • How many eligible customers used it.
  • Average betting days and amount staked per month.
  • Whether customers associated Betfair with rewarding loyalty.

In the 15-day trial, customers reported high satisfaction and rated the feature very easy to use. The boost pattern lasted: years later, I reused it for Your Odds+.
